AGROBRIDGE - THE PROCUREMENT CENTRE STORY (team pitch note)
============================================================

Model used: a wheat MSP mandi in Haryana in April (Punjab is almost identical; MP e-Uparjan
differences are noted at the end). Everything here comes from state procedures, news reports
and best-practice documents we studied; it is the "normal" case, and individual mandis vary.



PART A — THE REAL STORY TODAY (NO AGROBRIDGE)
=============================================

The people on the ground

  Who  |  What they are  |  Who they answer to
  Farmer  |  grows the crop, owns (or rents) the tractor-trolley  |  himself
  Arhtiya (commission agent)  |  licensed shopkeeper inside the mandi; has a phad (open floor) in front of his shop; employs a munshi (clerk); gets 2.5 % commission from the government per sale  |  licensed by the Market Committee
  Market Committee / Mandi Secretary  |  runs the yard: gate, roads, sheds, phads, auction platform, lighting, water, weighbridge at the gate; issues gate passes; licenses arhtiyas, weighmen and labour  |  HSAMB (state agri-marketing board)
  Procurement agency (FCI, HAFED, HSWC, Food & Supplies Dept.)  |  the buyer. Each mandi is allotted to one or more agencies, often on a day-rota  |  Government of India (FCI) / State (others)
  Quality Inspector (of the agency)  |  checks the heap against FAQ norms (moisture, foreign matter, damaged grain)  |  the agency
  Purchase Officer / Purchaser (of the agency)  |  records the purchase at MSP on e-Kharid  |  the agency
  Weighman (kanta wala)  |  operates the electronic platform scale on the phad  |  licensed by Market Committee; scale certified by Legal Metrology
  Palledars / mandi labour  |  unload, clean (sieve), heap, fill 50 kg bags, stitch, load trucks  |  labour union; rates fixed by Market Committee
  Transport contractor  |  trucks that lift bags from the mandi to the godown  |  tendered by the agency
  Godown (HSWC / FCI / CWC)  |  receives and stacks the bags  |  agency
  e-Kharid + MFMB  |  state portals: farmer register, gate pass, purchase (J-form), payment (DBT)  |  State Food & Supplies Dept. / NIC
  Bank / PFMS  |  pays the farmer  |  RBI / GoI

Step 0 — Before the season (Jan–Mar)
1. Farmer registers on Meri Fasal Mera Byora (MFMB): Aadhaar, mobile, bank account, khasra (field) numbers, crop, acres. Often the arhtiya or a CSC does it for him.
2. Patwari / Agriculture Dept verifies the crop and acres. This fixes the maximum quantity he can sell at MSP.
3. Record syncs to e-Kharid (24–48 h lag).

Step 1 — Getting a turn (the "schedule")
4. The Market Committee, through e-Kharid, issues a gate pass by SMS: mandi, date, sometimes a time slot. During peak (mid-April) these are rationed. Many farmers get the SMS a day before, some never; the arhtiya often decides who goes when.
5. No SMS → farmer either waits or comes anyway and asks the gate for a temporary pass (no purchase date).

Step 2 — Coming to the mandi
6. Farmer harvests, hires a tractor-trolley (₹1,500–2,500), loads 40–60 quintal loose, and drives.
7. Because nobody trusts the slot, most arrive the night before and queue on the road outside the gate. This is the congestion the PS talks about.

Step 3 — The gate (Market Committee)
8. Gate clerk checks the SMS / QR (2026: also biometric thumb), notes tractor number, and prints a gate-entry slip (the "token"). Regulated by the Market Committee.
9. Farmer drives to his arhtiya's phad (he already has a relationship — the arhtiya lent him money, sells him seed, etc.).

Step 4 — Unloading and heaping (labour, arhtiya's phad)
10. Palledars unload; grain lies in an open heap on the floor. Munshi notes farmer name, approx. quantity, gate slip number in the arhtiya's register.
11. If dirty/wet: labour sieves and spreads it to dry — hours to a day. Farmer waits (sleeps) beside his heap.

Step 5 — Quality check (Agency inspector)
12. The agency's Quality Inspector walks the phads on rounds (typically 2–3 times a day), pushes a moisture meter into the heap, checks for chaff and shrivelled grain against FAQ norms (wheat ≤ 12 % moisture, ≤ 0.75 % foreign matter, etc.).
13. Passes → heap is "cleared for purchase". Fails → dry it more / clean it / sell to a private trader below MSP. Nobody schedules these rounds against the heaps; farmer just waits for him to come.

Step 6 — Purchase entry (Agency purchaser + arhtiya)
14. Purchaser records the heap as bought at MSP (₹2,585/q wheat 2026) on e-Kharid under the arhtiya's login; the arhtiya's munshi does the actual data entry. This produces the "H-form" (purchase record).

Step 7 — Bagging and weighing (labour + weighman, arhtiya's scale)
15. Agency supplies bardana (jute bags). Palledars fill 50 kg each, stitch.
16. Weighman weighs bags in stacks on the arhtiya's electronic scale (owned by arhtiya, licensed and stamped by Legal Metrology, weighman licensed by Market Committee). Total quintals go into e-Kharid → I-form (weighment slip).
17. J-form (sale bill) is generated: farmer, quantity, rate, arhtiya, agency. This is the legal proof of sale.

Step 8 — Payment (Agency → PFMS → bank)
18. Agency approves the J-form batch; money goes by DBT to the farmer's bank (Haryana: promised 48–72 h; Punjab: arhtiya clicks "pay now" within 48 h, else direct after 72 h). Deductions for cleaning/labour are settled with the arhtiya. In bad years payment is held until the wheat is lifted and reaches the godown.
19. Farmer gets a bank SMS; no other status.

Step 9 — Lifting (Agency's transport contractor)
20. Filled bags sit on the phad until the contractor's truck comes (rule: within 72 h; reality: 5–15 days at peak). Palledars load; truck is weighed on the gate weighbridge (Market Committee's dharam kanta); goes to the godown.
21. Until lifted, the bags occupy the floor, so the next farmers cannot unload → gate passes are stopped → road jams. This is the hidden cause behind most "long waiting".


WHERE THE WAITING ACTUALLY COMES FROM
=====================================
- Turns rationed by SMS, arriving a day early because the SMS is not trusted (Steps 1–2).
- One gate, manual/biometric checks, portal outages (Step 3).
- Inspector rounds not matched to arrivals (Step 5).
- Floor full because lifting is slow; bardana/labour shortages (Steps 7, 9).
- Farmer has zero visibility after he unloads; asks the munshi every hour (Steps 4–8).



PART B — THE SAME STORY WITH AGROBRIDGE
=======================================

AgroBridge changes none of the roles above. It replaces only the quota gate-pass SMS with a
capacity-based booked hour, and it gives every step a timestamp so the farmer, the arhtiya and the
admin see the same picture. Government systems (MFMB, e-Kharid, DBT) are not touched; where their
data is needed we use a mock adapter that shows exactly where the real feed would plug in.

HOW THE INPUTS COME IN — WITHOUT EXTRA WORK FOR ANYONE
======================================================
Every number the engine uses is a side-effect of a tap someone already has to make:
- Gate scan → arrival time, vehicles.
- Assay Pass → assay time, grade.
- Weigh → actual quintals (corrects the farmer's guess; trains the correction model).
- Paid / Lifted → dwell times, floor occupancy.
- Farmer's own booking → lead time and history (trains the no-show model).
From these the engine learns per mandi: real quintals/hour (Model B), quantity correction (Model A),
no-show probability (Model C), and the yard factor. Nobody fills a form for the model.


WHAT STAYS EXACTLY AS IT IS
===========================
Arhtiya relationship and commission, agency rota, FAQ norms and the inspector's authority,
Legal-Metrology scales, bardana, labour rates, e-Kharid as the legal record, DBT as the payment.
AgroBridge is the scheduling and visibility layer; the day it gets a real e-Kharid/MFMB API, the
mock adapters in services/govRail.js are swapped and the screens do not change.


DIFFERENCES IN OTHER STATES (FOR THE PITCH)
===========================================
- Punjab (Anaaj Kharid): same roles; arhtiya even more central (he registers the farmer and receives payment first in some years). Our arhtiya bulk booking matches this.
- MP (e-Uparjan): no arhtiya; farmer books centre + date on the portal himself with a daily farmer cap; payment 7 working days. Our farmer self-booking matches this; arhtiya role simply unused.
- e-NAM mandis: same stage order (gate entry → assay → auction → weigh → pay); our stage chain lines up module-for-module.


WHAT HAPPENS TO GRAIN THE AGENCY WILL NOT BUY
Lots that fail FAQ (wet, dirty) or crops with no MSP (tomato, onion) cannot go to FCI/HAFED. Today the farmer
sells them on the spot to whoever offers. In AgroBridge such a lot is opened for a timed e-auction among
licensed traders (test accounts 9300000001-02); the farmer sees the bids and accepts or rejects. Same
e-NAM pattern, same yard capacity rules for the trader's trucks.
